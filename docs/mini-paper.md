# CF₃NO and restricted-reference checks

This companion note records the CF₃NO development study. The maintained [methods manuscript (PDF)](paper/pyscf-esd.pdf) covers the current methods and benchmark set, including finite-temperature anharmonicity and naphthalene phosphorescence.

## Abstract

PySCF ESD is a Python package for computing vibronic absorption and emission from a molecular geometry or from supplied electronic surfaces. The workflow combines the optimization of two electronic states, root and phase tracking, and a nuclear calculation that can retain anharmonic motion. Numerical controls test the derivatives, transition strengths and restricted orbital stability independently. In an H₂ control, rotating an internally unstable restricted reference recovered the ground-state solution, whereas stretched H₂ failed the restricted-to-unrestricted stability check. For the visible $n\to\pi^*$ transition of CF₃NO, PBE gave a stable restricted reference at the ground-state minimum, but the spin-breaking instability persisted near the excited-state stationary point. A separate 298 K rotor calculation on retained PBE0 inputs placed the absorption maximum 498.50 cm⁻¹ above the calculated origin, close to the experimental offset of 499.90 cm⁻¹, while underestimating the absolute maximum by 760.74 cm⁻¹. Its electronic reference failed the stability check, so the result is a numerical diagnostic of the reduced nuclear model.

## Introduction

A vertical excitation energy describes an electronic gap at one geometry. A vibronic band adds nuclear motion on both electronic surfaces, populations on the initial surface, and changes in the transition dipole with geometry. For a weak transition with a large-amplitude coordinate, a model may place the strongest absorption near the measured band while giving an inaccurate origin or progression. The visible band of trifluoronitrosomethane provides a case where vertical excitation, the cold origin and the room-temperature envelope can be assessed separately. [Hage et al.](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c11480)

PySCF ESD connects electronic-structure calculations to time-independent sums over vibrational states. The default molecular workflow starts from one XYZ geometry and targets the lowest singlet excitation of a closed-shell molecule at 0 K using PBE0/def2-TZVPD. The Python interface also accepts prepared electronic and vibrational inputs, including signed transition dipoles on a periodic grid. These interfaces allow electronic and nuclear approximations to be changed and tested at different stages of the same calculation. [Sun et al.](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0006074) · [Hirata and Head-Gordon](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0009-2614(99)01149-5) · [Adamo and Barone](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.478522) · [Rappoport and Furche](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3484283)

## Computational methods

The workflow optimizes the ground and selected excited geometries with analytic gradients and evaluates the electronic state along the optimization path. A signed overlap of particle–hole transition densities, evaluated with atomic-orbital overlaps between geometries, guides root assignment and places the transition dipoles in a common electronic phase. The implementation tests the self-consistent field (SCF) and Tamm–Dancoff approximation (TDA) residuals independently. At the minima, the native orbital stability calculation checks rotations within the restricted space and directions toward an unrestricted reference. An internal failure can trigger a fresh restricted SCF seeded by the unstable rotation. Acceptance requires a lower energy, the original SCF residual tolerance and a stable solution. When a user supplies alternate functionals, an instability toward spin breaking can trigger a complete calculation with the next functional, starting from the original input geometry. [Seeger and Pople](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.434318)

In the adiabatic Hessian model, a proper mass-weighted alignment removes relative translation and rotation before constructing the affine normal-coordinate map $Q_f=JQ_i+K$. Here $Q_i$ and $Q_f$ are the mass-weighted initial and final coordinates, $J$ is the Duschinsky rotation and $K$ is the displacement. With $E_s^{\min}$ the electronic energy at the minimum of surface $s$ and $Z_s$ its harmonic zero-point energy, the signed gap is $E_{00}=E_f^{\min}-E_i^{\min}+Z_f-Z_i$. The separately available vertical-gradient model uses the ground-state curvature on both surfaces. For isolated singlet roots in the supported restricted Hartree–Fock configuration-interaction singles (RHF–CIS) setting, the package computes the excited-state Hessian and the transition dipole derivative analytically. For density-functional calculations, it obtains them from checked local fits to analytic excited-state gradients and signed dipoles, respectively. [Santoro et al.](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2437197) · [Maurice and Head-Gordon](https://doi.org/10.1080/00268979909483096)

For initial and final vibrational eigenfunctions $\chi_{in}$ and $\chi_{fm}$ with vibrational quantum labels $n$ and $m$, the line strength in the harmonic model is

$$
S_{mn}=\left\lVert
\left\langle\chi_{fm}\left|
\boldsymbol\mu_0+\sum_k Q_{i,k}\,\boldsymbol\mu'_k
\right|\chi_{in}\right\rangle
\right\rVert^2 .
$$

The constant dipole $\boldsymbol\mu_0$ and the derivatives $\boldsymbol\mu'_k$ share the phase and coordinate frame of the expansion at $Q_i=0$. The Franck–Condon and linear Herzberg–Teller amplitudes therefore interfere before squaring. Each line carries the Boltzmann population of its initial level. Absorption oscillator strengths scale as $E_\gamma S_{mn}$ and spontaneous emission rates as $E_\gamma^3 S_{mn}$, with $E_\gamma$ the positive photon energy. The harmonic calculation checks initial population coverage and final dipole closure separately. Coverage is a property of the retained basis within the selected model. [Baiardi, Bloino and Barone](https://doi.org/10.1021/ct400450k)

Automatic anharmonic treatment uses electronic cuts on both surfaces. A one-mode system can be solved by an open-boundary discrete variable representation (DVR) on a common coordinate with a varying vector dipole. The automatic multimode treatment is local, with a truncated vibrational configuration-interaction space, selected intermode couplings and linear Herzberg–Teller terms. It qualifies anharmonic corrections at 0 K. A separate API accepts a periodic rotor and a harmonic bath and includes the thermal harmonic spectators through a characteristic function. [Light, Hamilton and Lill](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.448462)

## Numerical controls

For RHF/STO-3G H₂ at 1.4 bohr, the restricted antibonding determinant started at 0.4605764622 $E_h$. Stability rotation followed by a new SCF gave −1.1167143251 $E_h$, within $5\times10^{-16}$ $E_h$ of an independent ground-state SCF, with a residual of $1.24\times10^{-13}$ $E_h$. At 4.5 bohr, the workflow rejected the restricted reference in the spin-breaking channel. In a separate RHF–CIS/6-31G test on LiH, the analytic excited-state Hessian differed by at most $3.10\times10^{-8}$ $E_h$/bohr² from central differences of analytic gradients at a displacement of 0.002 bohr. For a synthetic periodic rotor with one harmonic spectator at 298 K, the integrated absolute difference between characteristic-function and direct stick broadening was below $3\times10^{-5}$ of the total photon-weighted strength in each direction.

The full one-geometry H₂ calculation at the default electronic level recovered more than 99.5% of the dipole closure sum in each direction. Its calculated B–X origin was 92,212.94 cm⁻¹ against the tabulated 90,203.35 cm⁻¹ (+2.23%). The first vibrational interval on B was 1,845.21 cm⁻¹ against 1,318.32 cm⁻¹ (+39.97%), obtained from the difference of the experimental P(1) lines. The calculation used average atomic masses and the experimental references refer to ¹H₂. [Huber and Herzberg compilation](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C1333740&Mask=1000) · [Hollenstein et al.](https://doi.org/10.1088/0953-4075/39/8/L02)

## CF₃NO at 298 K

At the reoptimized ground-state minimum of neutral CF₃NO in vacuum, PBE/def2-TZVPD with def2-universal-JKFIT and the GPU4PySCF level-4 integration grid gave twelve positive harmonic frequencies, the lowest at 78.628 cm⁻¹. The lowest restricted-to-unrestricted orbital Hessian eigenvalue was +0.00393723 in the native convention. Near the minimum on the tracked $n\to\pi^*$ surface, the maximum raw gradient was $1.203\times10^{-5}$ $E_h$/bohr, above the $10^{-5}$ $E_h$/bohr threshold. The maximum internally projected gradient was $1.082\times10^{-6}$ $E_h$/bohr. A body-frame stability calculation at this geometry gave +0.74748193 for the internal channel, +0.18278062 for the real-to-complex channel and −0.01741351 for the restricted-to-unrestricted channel. The negative spin-breaking eigenvalue prevented the calculation of an excited-state Hessian and periodic electronic surfaces at PBE. [Perdew, Burke and Ernzerhof](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.77.3865) · [Weigend](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcc.20702) · [Li et al.](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpca.4c05876)

The 298 K numerical control used earlier PBE0/TDA/def2-TZVPD inputs with negative restricted-to-unrestricted eigenvalues at both endpoints. The reduced model used frozen-spectator torsional cuts, one constant effective inertia per surface, eleven harmonic spectator modes and a common signed vector dipole sampled along the rotor at an intermediate spectator geometry. Independent Boltzmann distributions on the two initial surfaces furnished absorption and emission. The rotor partition function included all symmetry sectors with unit nuclear-spin statistical weights. The calculation retained its native photon energies and used a Gaussian full width at half maximum of 80 cm⁻¹. Halving the wavenumber step from 1 to 0.5 cm⁻¹ moved either maximum by less than 0.0015 cm⁻¹.

The calculated origin was 13,170.36 cm⁻¹, 759.34 cm⁻¹ below the jet-cooled value of 13,929.70 cm⁻¹. The absorption maximum at 13,668.86 cm⁻¹ was 760.74 cm⁻¹ below the 14,429.60 cm⁻¹ maximum point digitized from the room-temperature absorption used by Hage et al. (−5.27%). The two peak-to-origin offsets differ by 1.40 cm⁻¹. That comparison combines a cold origin with a warm band maximum and tests one interval within the envelope. Calculated emission peaked at 12,768.13 cm⁻¹, giving a calculated separation of the two maxima of 900.73 cm⁻¹. The 298 K experimental comparison covers absorption. [Mason and Banus](https://doi.org/10.1039/JR9570003904) · [Dyet, McCoustra and Pfab](https://doi.org/10.1039/F29888400463) · [Hage et al.](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c11480)

A separate pilot used state-averaged complete active-space self-consistent field (SA-CASSCF) with ten electrons in seven orbitals, the def2-SVP basis, and two equally weighted singlets. The atomic valence active space procedure targeted N/O 2p orbitals. The electronic calculation and analytic S₁ gradient response converged at both retained PBE geometries. The minimum singular value of the cross-geometry active-space overlap was 0.498, and the near-stationary PBE excited geometry had a maximum SA-CASSCF gradient of 0.05370 $E_h$/bohr. A spin-adapted spectral calculation requires its own relaxation and active-space tracking. [Sayfutyarova et al.](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.7b00128)

## Conclusions

The H₂ and CF₃NO controls illustrate different outcomes of the stability test. Restricted orbital rotation can recover a lower-energy determinant from an internal saddle, whereas the instability toward spin breaking can persist even when the tracked excited geometry is nearly stationary. A molecular CF₃NO benchmark at 298 K requires one consistent set of qualified surfaces, curvatures and phase-continuous dipoles. The package reports electric-dipole radiative quantities for the modeled ensemble. A measured lifetime or quantum yield additionally requires population kinetics and nonradiative channels.
